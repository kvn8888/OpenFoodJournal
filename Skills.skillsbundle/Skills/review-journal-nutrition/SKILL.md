---
name: review-journal-nutrition
description: Review what someone actually recorded in OpenFoodJournal for a meal, day, or date range, compare logged nutrition across periods, identify foods contributing recorded nutrients, and report existing nutrition targets, settings, or active energy when asked.
---

Use the journal as the authoritative source for previously logged intake, not today's Food Bank recipe values or general nutrition estimates.

- For totals or daily averages, prefer **Read Journal Nutrition Summary**. Pass the exact inclusive local calendar dates and meal filter, if requested. Compare like periods using separate summary calls; never compare a single meal to a full day as if they were equivalent. The summary's daily average divides by all calendar days in its range; report tracked days alongside the average when coverage is incomplete. Missing days are not evidence that the person ate nothing.
- For questions about which foods were logged or how much a particular food contributed, use **Read Journal Entries**, with the same dates and filters. Read further pages until the reported total count is accounted for when complete results are needed. Use the portion actually logged and its recorded nutrients. Do not substitute current Food Bank values for past entries.
- If the person asks what their existing goals are, use **Read Nutrition Goals**. For factual comparison with today's recorded intake, the full-day summary also reports the current goals; labeled remaining amounts apply only to a single full day. Goals are current settings, not historical goals. Do not set or change goals, prescribe restriction, or turn reported values into a weight-loss plan. Don't suggest skipping meals or compensating for food with exercise.
- **Read OpenFoodJournal Nutrition Settings** gives the currently configured meal windows and Food Shelf preferences. Meal-specific totals still come from the journal.
- Only if asked about recorded activity, use **Read Active Energy in OpenFoodJournal** for the specified day. This is active energy from Health, not total daily energy expenditure; a missing value means unavailable, not zero. Keep activity and intake separate.

Use kcal, grams, and the stored micronutrient units. Do not combine incompatible units; a missing micronutrient is unknown. For date ranges larger than 366 days, split into non-overlapping inclusive ranges and label coverage. If data is unavailable, say so. Do not fill gaps with model estimates, disclose unrelated personal data, or modify records while answering a read request.
